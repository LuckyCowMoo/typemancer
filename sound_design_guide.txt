================================================================================
TYPEMANCER - SOUND DESIGN & AUDIO SYNTHESIS GUIDE
A Guide for Autonomous Agents & Developers Working on Typemancer Audio
================================================================================

1. CORE GAME THEME & AESTHETIC IDENTITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Typemancer is an antiquarian, tactile, arcane typing roguelike deckbuilder.
The thematic pillar is: "Tactile Antiquarian Scholar meets Visceral Spellcraft."

* Key aesthetic motifs:
  - Worn parchment, dried vellum, quill pens, and iron gall ink.
  - Antiquarian study rooms: solid mahogany wood, brass clockwork, dusty tomes.
  - Mechanical typing: typewriter keybars, escapement clicks, return bells, chalk on slate.
  - Ancient magical subterranean realms: echoing stone, bubbling ink marshes, crystal bastions, volcanic caldera steam vents, quiet sunlit sanctuaries.

* Sound design philosophy:
  All sounds must feel grounded in real physical materials—wood, paper, stone,
  iron, brass, water, fire, and organic air—blended with celestial and mystical
  harmonics. Digital or arcade aesthetics break immersion immediately.


2. USER PREFERENCES & CRITICAL "WHAT TO AVOID"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Through iterative user feedback on sound design, clear rules and preferences
have been established. Follow these strictly:

[AVOID: 8-Bit & Chiptune Artifacts]
- Never use raw square waves, simple bleeps, or arcade coin chirps.
- Clocks, coins, and clicks must sound like authentic acoustic objects.
- Clocks should sound like antique wooden grandfather clocks with pendulum resonance,
  ticking on relaxed 1.5s–2.0s intervals, and occasionally "skipping a beat" like a real mechanical clockwork.
- Coins must sound like real metal discs rustling, clinking on wood, or jingling in leather pouches—not high-pitched digital dings.

[AVOID: Predictable, Rigid, or Mechanical Modulation]
- Never use simple fixed-frequency LFOs for wind, water, or background drones.
  The user can immediately spot obvious sine-wave volume oscillations.
- Modulate wind and water with randomized depth, drifting frequencies, and stochastic amplitude jitter.
- Avoid repetitive cyclic highlight cues (e.g. descending 3-note chime patterns repeating constantly).

[AVOID: Constant Monotonous Drones]
- Ethereal or mystical pads (Crystal Bastion, Magic Forest, Sunlit Sanctuary) must
  NEVER sit at a single unchanging frequency or steady volume in the foreground.
- They must breathe naturally: dynamic swell, gentle melodic movement, shifting
  intervals, and long quiet pauses so they do not cause listening fatigue.

[AVOID: Rapid Inhuman Speed]
- Writing sounds must reflect deliberate human handwriting on parchment, not a frantic,
  harsh machine-gun scribbling.
- Keep paper rustles, turns, and quill scratches paced and organic.

[DO: Dynamic Range & Restrained Backgrounds]
- Keep constant atmospheric background noise quiet and unobtrusive (so the player can focus on typing).
- Atmospheric highlights (falling pebbles in canyons, bursting ink bubbles, rare steam vents) should be distinct, infrequent, and obvious when they occur.
- Provide editor controls: ensure sounds can be auditioned cleanly and muted when editing gameplay parameters.


3. ARCHITECTURE & TOOLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Typemancer utilizes a dual audio architecture:

A. Offline DSP Physical Modeling Generator (`scripts/generate_sfx.cjs`)
   - Generates standalone, sample-accurate 44.1kHz 16-bit PCM RIFF WAV & OGG files.
   - Stored in `public/assets/sfx/`.
   - Used for one-shot typing strikes, card plays, blade slashes, anvil strikes,
     paper tears, chest opens, and victory/defeat stingers.
   - Can be run anytime via `node scripts/generate_sfx.cjs` and tested with `node scripts/verify_sfx.cjs`.

B. Real-Time Procedural Web Audio Engine (`src/utils/soundEngine.ts`)
   - Employs the browser's native `AudioContext` to construct real-time node graphs.
   - Combines oscillators, custom white/pink noise audio buffers, biquad filters,
     stereo panners, gain nodes, and seamless streaming buffer loops.
   - Generates endless, non-repeating dynamic biomes and non-battle room ambiances.
   - Handles randomized event scheduling (falling rocks, steam vents, bubbling marshes, grandfather clock ticks).


4. SYNTHESIS METHODS & MATHEMATICAL DSP RECIPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. Sound File Generation (Node.js Buffer Creation)
   - Writes valid 44-byte RIFF/WAVE headers followed by 16-bit signed PCM data:
     ```javascript
     const sample = Math.max(-1, Math.min(1, floatVal));
     buffer.writeInt16LE(Math.round(sample * 32767), 44 + i * 2);
     ```
   - Soft-clipping saturation via hyperbolic tangent: `Math.tanh(x)`.
   - Peak normalization with headroom (typically 0.88 to 0.95 peak).

B. Noise Types
   - White Noise: Uniform random `Math.random() * 2 - 1`.
   - Pink Noise: Paul Kellet's filtered noise algorithm (equal energy per octave)
     providing warm, organic basis for wind, fire, water, and paper.

C. Physical Modeling Envelopes & Filters
   - Exponential Decays: `Math.exp(-t * decayFactor)`.
   - Resonant Biquad Bandpass Filters: 2nd-order IIR filters modeling body cavities,
     pipes, slate surfaces, and wooden enclosures.
   - Modal Synthesis: Sum of inharmonic sinusoidal partials modeling bells, anvils,
     crystal fractures, and metallicize plate armor:
     - Anvil strikes: Tuned harmonics (D6, F#6, A6) with soft strike transients.
     - Ward shatter: High glass fracture noise + crystal modal partials (1420Hz, 2150Hz, 3380Hz, 4920Hz).
     - Defeat bell: Dark inharmonic low iron partials (110Hz, 147Hz, 222Hz, 335Hz, 460Hz) over a 42Hz sub-bass tremor.

D. Procedural Real-Time Web Audio Ambiances
   - Grandfather Clock:
     Two low resonant bandpass nodes (180Hz / 420Hz) stimulated by micro-transients,
     spaced at ~1.8s, with small random timing jitter and a ~6% chance to drop a beat.
   - Riverbank:
     Low-frequency pink noise filtered through resonant bands with randomized phase
     blended with authentic stony stream audio loop (`ambiance/river_stony.ogg`).
   - Campfire:
     Organic crackle and ember pops generated via sparse Poisson bursts blended with
     combustion roar and authentic wood-fire loop (`ambiance/campfire_loop.ogg`).
   - Rocky Desert Canyon:
     Dynamic wind howling modulated by low-frequency noise (not a sine LFO),
     paired with sporadic, randomized pebble cascades (decaying burst series).
   - Inking Marsh:
     Sub-bass swamp rumble (55Hz–75Hz) paired with dynamic envelope-shaped bubble
     inflation (rising pitch 140Hz -> 260Hz) followed by an acoustic burst "plop".
   - Steam Vents & Volcanic Caldera:
     Deep subterranean rumble with rare, prominent high-pressure hiss bursts
     (bandpass filtered noise sweeping 1200Hz to 3800Hz with fast attack and long vapor decay).


5. CHECKLIST FOR FUTURE AUDIO WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When tasked with creating, revising, or tuning sounds for Typemancer:
1. Does it sound acoustic and natural, or does it sound digital/8-bit?
   -> If it sounds like an 8-bit game, replace it with physical modeling or realistic samples.
2. Does the ambiance loop cause fatigue after 3 minutes of listening?
   -> Introduce stochastic volume variance, tone modulation, and breathing room.
3. Are atmospheric highlight events (pebbles, steam, bubbles) firing too frequently?
   -> Increase intervals, randomize timers, and vary pitch/volume per occurrence.
4. Are UI and card sounds tactile and satisfying to type with?
   -> Prioritize typewriter clicks, quill scratches, and clean mechanical thocks.
5. Did you test the sound inside the Dev Editor?
   -> Verify auditioning works cleanly and that sound effects respect the editor mute flags.
